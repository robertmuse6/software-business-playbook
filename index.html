What the train runs on

Software has to deliver after the pitch ends.

A gym, a coaching service and a software product can all need marketing and sales. They differ in how value is delivered, how often the job repeats and what it costs to serve one more customer.

Where Hormozi helps: offer clarity, distribution, prospect knowledge, objection diagnosis, sales training.

Where other evidence must lead: activation, product usage, cohort retention, reliability, privacy, billing and unit economics. A strong close is not evidence of a healthy software product.

One complete worked example · invented, not a case study

What could a tiny coaching product actually look like?

Suppose the job is “I need to compare two career moves and take one sensible next step.” This is a generic scenario, not a chosen audience or a promise of employment. Every stage below is a testable hypothesis.

1. Free unit

A 10-minute comparison sheet: two options, constraints, assumptions and one reversible real-world test. No account needed to see a completed example.

Distribution test: one useful public walkthrough shared in a relevant community with permission. Measure relevant starts and finished sheets, not viral reach.

2. Passive user

Someone voluntarily saves the tool or opts in, but has not completed a useful comparison. This definition is provisional.

Transition test: one clear next prompt: “Pick the two options you are weighing. Leave anything sensitive out.” Observe confusion before sending more reminders.

3. Active user

They finish a comparison and attempt one agreed real-world test, such as speaking to someone who does the work.

Value test: ask what changed in the decision. Check whether this milestone is associated with later useful action; do not assume the event causes retention.

4. Paid user

A bounded supported sprint could add feedback, a progress review and help interpreting what the test revealed. The offer has a finish date and clear limits.

Pricing test: ask for a real purchase only when delivery is possible. State exactly what is included. Count service time. No specific price or guaranteed outcome is selected.

5. Recurring core

If the person has an ongoing action cycle, a recurring workflow could help them plan, act, reflect and adjust. If the decision is finished, let the job end.

Retention test: compare users at the same age on repeated useful action. A subscription is appropriate only if the job and value recur.

The first automation

Start by making the comparison and next-step loop reliable. Add saved progress and basic events. Keep human judgment visible where it remains necessary.

Build gate: automate repeated friction observed in actual runs. A multi-engine platform or a sales hire waits until it solves a demonstrated bottleneck.

The actual words at one conversion point

Illustrative offer: “The free sheet helps you compare two options and choose a test. The supported sprint adds feedback on your plan and a review after you try it. It does not guarantee a job, an admission or a salary increase. Would that support be useful for the decision you described?”

If they say it sounds like ChatGPT: “Fair question. The test is whether this workflow and follow-through help you take a useful step that your current tools do not. If a general chatbot already solves the job for you, you may not need this.”

If they decline: ask once whether the issue is fit, timing, confidence or price, if they are willing to say. Accept the answer. No urgency is invented.

Every handoff has a distribution and pricing question.

Use this as the weekly review. The answers are decisions to test, not facts about the current venture.

Free → passive

Reach: where does this urgent problem already get discussed?

Price: is free a resource, a trial or an ongoing plan?

Test one useful public example and one permitted channel. Track relevant arrivals and consented starts, not impressions alone.

Passive → active

Reach: can an in-product prompt or consented follow-up bring someone to the first useful task?

Price: can they experience enough value before a payment decision?

Watch the first run. Test one removed obstacle. Do not gate the entire useful experience merely to force a sales call.

Active → paid

Reach: who has a real next job after the free result?

Price: what specific capability or outcome makes the paid step worth buying?

Test a clear bounded offer with qualified users. Count actual payments and delivered value, not stated willingness alone.

Paid → recurring core

Reach: what natural customer trigger should bring them back?

Price: does a subscription match a repeated job, or should the product end?

Compare cohorts at the same age. Separate successful completion, lost value and failed payments before trying retention messages.

Core → advocacy

Reach: why would a satisfied user introduce a similar buyer?

Price: can any reward or partner fee be funded without erasing contribution?

Ask for a consensual introduction only after a useful outcome. Do not expose personal coaching results or assume viral growth.

Across all stages

Owner: who sees the handoff fail and can fix it?

Learning: which evidence would make you change the offer or model?

Use raw counts, a named observation window, channel tags and one next experiment. No generic conversion percentage is a target here.

Your latest question changes the spine.

The current path is free offer → passive → active → paid → core recurring. Each transition asks about pricing, distribution, product value and the business rails underneath it.

“Passive,” “active” and “core” are still to be defined for the chosen job. A signup is not automatically activation. A recurring subscription is not proof of recurring need.

The public examples stay generic. Product direction, audience and pricing are not fixed, and private research details are not published here.
